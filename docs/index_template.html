The Primacy Premium

Conditional forecasts of defense and commercial markets under Chinese military primacy, 2026 to 2035

alyssa i. agard

❦

Three pathways to Chinese military primacy by 2035 are simulated from parameters estimated on IMF and SIPRI data. This page summarizes the design, the four static figures, and the results. The interactive page and the report give the full account.

§1 research question and design

If China reaches nuclear parity with the United States and conventional military primacy in the Indo-Pacific by 2035, how do defense expenditure, arms transfers, and reserve currency composition change, and do the changes depend on the pathway by which primacy is reached?

The project specifies three pathways and simulates each from parameters estimated on historical data. Results are distributions conditional on the pathway. The pathways are assumptions, not forecasts of what will occur: the historical record contains no instance of the outcome, so response parameters are estimated from observed episodes and each pathway is imposed.

thesis.  Allied defense expenditure and reserve composition in 2035 depend on how Chinese primacy is reached (gradual accretion, US retrenchment, or a military demonstration), not only on whether it is reached.

§2 three pathways

I

Accretion

Chinese and US real expenditure continue at their compound growth rates of the last ten years of data.

{{kpis.cagr_start}} to {{kpis.year}}: China {{kpis.cn_cagr|+f1}}% and the United States {{kpis.us_cagr|+f1}}% per year.

II

Retrenchment

Chinese expenditure as in accretion. US real expenditure contracts at the rate observed from 1988 to 1998.

{{peace_dividend.peak_year}} to {{peace_dividend.trough_year}}: {{peace_dividend.annual_rate_pct|f1}}% per year.

III

Demonstration

Accretion, plus one event in a year drawn from 2028 to 2031. Reserve shares and allied expenditure growth repeat the changes observed in 2022.

One episode: the reserve share changes of 2022-Q1 and 2022-Q2, and the change in allied growth after February 2022.

The pathways share the premise of Chinese primacy by 2035. They do not impose an identical 2035 force balance: US expenditure in 2035 is lower under retrenchment.

§4 method

  1. Data. Seven parsers convert published sources to long-format tables with range checks. The COFER dollar share is recomputed from claims and must match the published share within 0.05 percentage points (parse_cofer.py). SIPRI expenditure is aggregated to six tiers, with dollar totals chain-linked over matched members (parse_milex.py).
  2. Estimation. The quarterly change in the dollar share is regressed on the year-on-year change in the log China/US expenditure ratio with Newey-West standard errors; the renminbi share is modeled in log-odds (calibrate.py). Allied tier growth is regressed on lagged US and Chinese growth, and H2 is tested on a country panel with fixed effects and Driscoll-Kraay standard errors (calibrate_layer2.py).
  3. Simulation. {{meta.draws|int}} seeded draws per pathway (simulate.py, simulate_layer2.py). Coefficients are drawn jointly from their estimated sampling distributions, innovations are resampled from estimated residuals, and the event date is random.
  4. Hypothesis assembly. Statistics and evidence series are written to hypothesis_panel.csv and verdicts to hypothesis_tests.json.
  5. Output. Four figures (make_figures.py), the interactive page, this summary (build_preview.py), and the report. The processed tables load directly into Power BI or Tableau.

Both event responses repeat one episode (2022). Reserve shares are valued at current exchange rates and mix transactions with valuation changes. The tier equations behind the expenditure simulation are static, with serially correlated residuals. The H1 statistic is descriptive.

§7 observable checks

Observations that would contradict the model's assumptions or the interpretation of its results:

  • Sustained allied expenditure growth during a US contraction.
  • A falling US share of allied arms imports during a US contraction.
  • Convergence of the renminbi's payments and reserve shares without capital account liberalization.
  • A further decline in the dollar share absorbed mainly by the renminbi.
  • War risk premium changes during Taiwan Strait exercises that differ materially from the 2026 Mideast Gulf multiple ({{hyp.war_multiple}}).

§8 scope

Simulated

  • US dollar and renminbi reserve shares, quarterly to 2035-Q4
  • Expenditure of six blocks, annually to 2035
  • World arms deliveries, projected from simulated world expenditure

Measured, not simulated

  • Renminbi payments and trade finance shares, {{vintage.swift_issues}} Swift issues
  • TSMC revenue, shipping structure, oil chokepoint flows, Brent price
  • War risk premium in the 2026 Mideast Gulf episode
