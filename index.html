The Primacy Premium.

Conditional forecasts of defense and commercial markets under Chinese military primacy, 2026 to 2035

Three pathways to Chinese military primacy by 2035 are simulated from parameters estimated on historical data. The project tests whether allied defense expenditure and reserve currency composition depend on the pathway.

Research question and design
§1

If China reaches nuclear parity with the United States and conventional military primacy in the Indo-Pacific by 2035, how do defense expenditure, arms transfers, and reserve currency composition change, and do the changes depend on the pathway by which primacy is reached?

The project specifies three pathways and simulates each from parameters estimated on historical data. Results are distributions conditional on the pathway. The pathways are assumptions, not forecasts of what will occur. The historical record contains no instance of the outcome, so no model can be fitted to it directly. Response parameters are therefore estimated from observed episodes, and each pathway is imposed.

Two outcomes are simulated: military expenditure by block, and the US dollar and renminbi shares of official foreign exchange reserves. Three further channels (semiconductors, shipping and war risk insurance, and energy) are documented with measured exposure series and are not simulated.

thesis.  Allied defense expenditure and reserve composition in 2035 depend on how Chinese primacy is reached (gradual accretion, US retrenchment, or a military demonstration), not only on whether it is reached.
Three pathways
§2

Each pathway is defined by growth rates or an event taken from a measured historical episode. The pathways share the premise of Chinese primacy by 2035. They do not impose an identical 2035 force balance: US expenditure in 2035 is lower under retrenchment than under the other two pathways.

I

Accretion

Chinese and US real expenditure continue at their compound growth rates of the last ten years of data.

II

Retrenchment

Chinese expenditure as in accretion. US real expenditure contracts at the rate observed from 1988 to 1998.

III

Demonstration

Accretion, plus one event in a year drawn from 2028 to 2031. Reserve shares and allied expenditure growth repeat the changes observed in 2022.

Results
§3
    Method
    §4

    The pipeline has five stages. Each stage reads only the outputs of earlier stages.

    1. Data. Seven parsers convert published sources to long-format tables: IMF COFER (parse_cofer.py); the SIPRI expenditure workbook, including tier and country panels (parse_milex.py); the SIPRI arms transfer tables and trade register (parse_arms_transfers.py); monthly Swift tracker issues (parse_swift.py); four UNCTAD maritime tables (parse_maritime.py); the EIA chokepoint report, the Short-Term Energy Outlook, and TSMC quarterly statements (parse_energy_semis.py); and NATO, nuclear, arms import, event, and alliance tables (parse_security.py). Extracted values are range-checked and the build stops on a failed check. The COFER dollar share is recomputed from claims and must match the published share within 0.05 percentage points.
    2. Estimation. The reserve channel is a regression of the quarterly change in the dollar share on the year-on-year change in the log China/US expenditure ratio and an indicator for 2022-Q1 and 2022-Q2, with Newey-West standard errors (four lags); the renminbi share is modeled the same way in log-odds (calibrate.py). For each allied tier, real growth is regressed on the previous year's US and Chinese growth, to , with Newey-West standard errors (three lags). H2 is tested on a country panel with fixed effects and Driscoll-Kraay standard errors (calibrate_layer2.py).
    3. Simulation. draws per pathway. Reserve shares are simulated quarterly to 2035-Q4 (simulate.py). Expenditure is simulated annually to 2035 for six blocks: the United States, China, three allied tiers, and the rest of the world (simulate_layer2.py). Coefficients are drawn jointly from their estimated sampling distributions. Innovations are resampled from estimated residuals; expenditure residuals are resampled in runs of consecutive years, jointly across blocks. The event date in the demonstration pathway is random.
    4. Hypothesis assembly. Test statistics, evidence series, and context are written to hypothesis_panel.csv, and verdicts to hypothesis_tests.json (build_hypothesis_panel.py).
    5. Output. Four static figures (make_figures.py), this page (build_dashboard_data.py, build_index.py), a static summary, a report, and a data dictionary. The processed tables load directly into Power BI or Tableau.
    Observable checks
    §5

    Observations that would contradict the model's assumptions or the interpretation of its results.

    • Allied expenditure after a US contraction. The tier equations imply lower allied growth after lower US growth. Sustained allied growth during a US contraction, as in , contradicts them.
    • Supplier concentration of allied arms imports. A falling US share of allied imports during a US contraction would indicate substitution away from US supply, which the model does not represent.
    • The renminbi's payments share relative to its reserve share. H3 requires the payments share to remain above the reserve share. Convergence without capital account liberalization would contradict the interpretation given here.
    • The reserve share of currencies other than the dollar, euro, yen, pound, and renminbi. H3 requires these currencies, not the renminbi, to absorb most of any further decline in the dollar share.
    • War risk premiums during Taiwan Strait exercises. The demonstration pathway attaches the 2026 Mideast Gulf premium multiple () to its event. Premium changes during future exercises provide a direct comparison.
    Limitations
    §6

    Estimation

    • The regressors in H2 vary only over time, so the effective sample is years. The result depends on expenditure weighting.
    • The tier equations are static. Their residuals are serially correlated, most strongly for NATO Europe, and most coefficient intervals include zero.
    • The renminbi model is estimated on quarters. Its simulated intervals are wide.
    • COFER shares are valued at current exchange rates and mix transactions with valuation changes.
    • Chinese expenditure figures are SIPRI estimates. SIPRI does not report the United Arab Emirates after 2014.

    Simulation

    • The reserve and expenditure responses to a demonstration event each repeat a single episode (2022).
    • The H1 statistic is descriptive. US expenditure differs across pathways by assumption, and the threshold of twice the reserve separation is a convention.
    • Rest-of-world growth is set to its mean over the last ten years of data, a period that includes wartime expenditure growth in Russia and Ukraine.
    • Semiconductor, shipping, and energy exposure series are reported but not simulated. Foundry concentration is not computed because market share data are not available to the project.
